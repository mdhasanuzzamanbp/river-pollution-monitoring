Fig 1: Spatial variations of the mean physiochemical profiles in the three segments of up, lower and downstream river system, Bangladesh. The bar associated with the mean values represent standard deviations. Different letters (a, b, and c) at the top mean statistically significant spatial differences of the metals based on a one-way ANOVA test (p<0.05).

<br>
<div align="justify">
The basic statistics (mean and standard deviation) of physicochemical parameters in sediments are outlined in the table. The pH of the sediment varied within 6.5 to 7. Low pH in water can cause the release of absorbed metals, which are found in the upstream river (Mean: 6.23 ±0.76) because it reduces the strength of metal connection and interrupts the tension of metals in surface sediments. The maximum and minimum average pH values were estimated as 5.84-7.25 in the upstream river, 6.51-7.26 in the lower midstream river and 8.01-8.67 in the downstream river sediments, respectively. According to these findings, sediments were slightly alkaline to slightly acidic. The high percentage (%) of organic matter (OM) is detected in the upstream river (Mean: 2.63 ±0.54). The low percentage (%) of OM was found in the downstream river e.g., Pashur and Rupsha River (Mean: 1.32 ±0.91). The TOC in sediments was mainly averaged 1.87 ±0.54, 1.91 ±0.39 and 1.87 ±0.39 in upstream, lower midstream and downstream rivers, respectively.
</div>

<div align="justify">
The BCR-originating metal fractions influencing bioavailability and bioaccumulation are presented in Fig. 4.2a. All metals were predominantly concentrated in the exchangeable fraction (F1) except for Cd and Pb, which were found in the residual fraction (F4). Average Res. Cd and Res. Pb percentages were 43 and 36.9 % respectively while other metals exchangeable percentages were 41.77% 30 (Exc.Mn), 39.33% (Exc.Fe), 39.57% (Exc.Ni), 42.83% (Exc.Cr), 43.63 % (Exc.Zn), 41.4% (Exc.Cu), 42.37% (Exc.As) respectively (Fig. 2a). Fig 2b in general, metal exchangeable fraction contributions in the riverine sediment followed: Ni > Cr > Zn> As> Fe>Cu>Cd>Mn>Pb. Similarly, organic fraction contributions were in order: Fe>Cu>Mn>Cd>Zn>Ni>As>Cr>Pb. By contrast, metal residual fraction contributions are in the descending order: Pb>Cd>Cu>As>Zn>Fe>Mn>Ni>Cr. Likewise, reducible fraction contributions in the sediment followed: Mn>Cr>Pb>Fe>Zn>As>Ni>Cd>Cu.

  Fig 9: Measured and predicted metal concentrations (mg Kg-1) in surface sediments at significant level (p < 0.01) (Left panels). Black and red lines represent measured and predicted concentrations. Linear regression results are related to a one-to-one function line (y=x) in right panels. Red line denotes linear regression outcomes whereas black dashed line signifies one-to-one function.

<div align="justify">
Predicted metal datasets were compared to measured metal datasets based on the PMF model the fig. The r2 values were utilized to appraise the efficiency of the PMF model predictions. All metals depicted rational linear regression outcomes with r2 values higher than 0.592. The greater r2 values were detected for Cd (0.996), Mn (0.995), Fe (0.969) and Cr (0.828), demonstrating that the metal datasets forecasted by the preferred source factors were well described in the PMF model. The r2 values for Zn were only 0.137 when all datasets were considered; however, it was strongly affected by a single outlier at location S13 (631.4 mg kg−1 and 4 times higher than the mean Zn concentration)
